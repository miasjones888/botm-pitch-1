# Reference cluster

Forty visual references curated to demonstrate the visual language in finished work. These are not the work — they are the *atmospheric and compositional neighborhood* the work lives in.

**This cluster contains two registers.** The visual language operates in both, depending on format:

### Register A — Quiet Chapbook Restraint
*Primary mode for the zine and for any long-form editorial work.* Single-image-per-page composition, generous cream margins, quiet typography, white space treated as composition. References 10, 13, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20 from the original batch — and references 25, 28, 36, 38, 40 from the expanded batch — are strongest for this mode.

### Register B — Cinematic-Editorial Composite
*Primary mode for the pitch deck, film direction, and social cuts.* Composed photography with typographic overlay, layered film-strip moments, handwritten epistolary elements, duotone treatments. References 1, 2, 8 from the original batch — and references 21, 22, 29, 30, 31, 32, 35, 37, 39 from the expanded batch — are strongest for this mode.

The registers share: cream or warm-white grounds, quiet typography (never serif display), duotone photo treatments, generous negative space, editorial restraint over collage chaos, handwriting used as small punctuation rather than decoration.

The registers differ on: image density (Register A uses one per page, Register B layers several), hand element (Register A uses hand once only, Register B uses handwriting as epistolary voice), typographic overlay (Register A keeps type and image in separate fields, Register B permits type over image for subtitle-grammar moments).

**Both registers reject:** full-color flatlay photography, serif display type, busy tape/rotation/overlap collage, pure black or pure white grounds, scattered decorative marginalia, Kinfolk/lifestyle/feminine-domestic register.

## Batch 02 · Expanded (21–40)

### Film, deck, and social targets

**21 · FILM · portrait cinematic callouts — subject study** — Silhouetted figure with anatomical callout boxes (eyes, hands, clothing detail). *This is the model for how the reader-figure is built in the film — visible through fragments, held as a composed silhouette.* Reference for deck slide 3's drift-movement grid and for the film's "three modalities of the reader-figure" (cinematic body / casual body / written word).

**22 · EPISTOLARY · postcard to random friend — handwritten** — Full postcard with duotone window image on top, long handwritten letter on bottom, vintage stamp. *Strong reference for deck slide 5 (postcard close) and for the film's epistolary voice-over register.* The handwriting here is more than a signature — it's a letter, which is a permitted Register B use.

**23 · FILM · atmosphere — shadow, water sparkle** — Two film stills stacked: shadow on sand, water sparkle with reeds. *Direct reference for the "atmosphere / the held shot" grammar of the film.* Camera rests. Nothing narrates. The image is the meaning.

**24 · EDITORIAL · ring-bound deck — mono caption format** — Deck spread with typewriter-style mono caption on top, full-bleed street photo on bottom, in a literal ring binder. *Strong reference for the deck's mono-caption-plus-image format and for the "pitch deck as artifact" feeling.*

**25 · FILM · Atonement cream poster — cut-out silhouette** — Two film stills: a girl shape cut out of stone steps, Atonement film poster with girl on cream. *Model for cream-ground film poster treatment and for the cut-out silhouette as a compositional move.*

**26 · MOODBOARD · misty tree composite — atmosphere** — Layered composite of misty tree photographs, grainy, painterly. *Reference for the emotional register of the film's photography — soft focus, mist, Morandi-like color, quietness.*

**27 · PROCESS · memories mindmap — handwritten arrows** — Working document with black-and-white photos, handwritten caption strips, hand-drawn arrows showing emotional flow. *Reference material for the internal-process feeling that underlies the film — memory as wandering.* Not a target layout.

**28 · ZINE · consciousness — cutout captions on cream** — Zine spread with tree image, typewriter-strip caption "consciousness of that and try to be better," Korean text running alongside. *Strong reference for zine spread that uses image + pulled quote in cutout strip.*

**29 · EPISTOLARY · post-it handwritten over Paris** — A yellow post-it with handwritten line ("I need the silence, I need the empty streets") floating over a Paris rooftop photograph. *Model for the film's text-as-written-word-over-image modality — a line appears, sits on the image, stays.* Crucial reference for Register B's typographic overlay logic.

**30 · EDITORIAL · life is short diptych — manuscript + cliff** — Two panels: vintage handwritten manuscript on left, cliff landscape with small pink type saying "life is short and the world is wide" on right. *Direct reference for how text-over-image works when the text is the writer's interior voice* — small, precise placement, not dominant.

**31 · DECK · Films & tv torn-paper blue duotone collage** — Layout with film strip down left edge, cyan-blue torn-paper collage with cursive display serif "Films & tv" title, body text underneath. *This reference permits one specific move the system normally rejects: torn-edge collage.* Only to be used when the subject *is* cinematic memory or film criticism. The torn edges read as film strip / cut film, not as scrapbook decoration.

**32 · ZINE · winter bear — pressed flower + film stills** — Complex layered spread: pressed blue flower, three film stills in a column, typewriter body text, handwritten "Winter Bear" signature. *Strong reference for the zine's most complex spread — the one where several moves combine to form a lyric collage.* Note: this is denser than the default zine register, but every element is still placed with intention and surrounded by cream.

**33 · JOURNAL · splatter, handwriting, purple aura** — Notebook spread with ink splatter, small handwritten line "to touch upon what's not yet known," purple light gradient right page. *Reference material for the work's spiritual-theoretical register* — the space where writing meets not-yet-known. Not a target layout.

**34 · ZINE · De Botanica — embroidered flower on bookcloth** — Vintage zine cover: green bookcloth, photograph of flowers with embroidered thread outlines and typeset cut-out captions. *Reference for how tactile handcraft can coexist with editorial discipline.* The embroidery is precise, the captions are letterpress-quality. Not DIY-looking.

**35 · SOCIAL · internet can't archive — Polaroid + doodle** — Polaroid of two women running in a field with handwritten "The internet can't archive this feeling" above and below, small doodled flowers. *Strong reference for the short-cut / social post register.* A vertical, single-moment, caption-forward cut. Hand element used as framing, not as scribble.

**36 · DECK · forest — mono typography + still on cream** — Video player-looking image on left (forest footage), cream right page with the single word "forest" in monospace. *Strongest single reference for how the deck or a title card handles the image-plus-word composition.* This is what the deck's title slides should feel like.

**37 · ZINE · wildflower grid — blue ground botanical** — Multi-panel botanical spread with blue risograph ground, layered flower photographs. *Reference for the zine's botanical grid spread* — Risograph-like treatment, color blocking, placed botanical photography. More saturated than the default zine register, but still disciplined.

**38 · ZINE · Sugimoto sky / seascape grid — density** — Nine-panel grid of sky and seascape fragments at different times of day. *Direct reference for zine brief Spread 08 (Catalog).* Stronger than reference 10 because each panel is compositionally complete rather than just documentary.

**40 · ZINE · grass + ocean diptych on cream mat** — Simple two-panel photograph: grass meadow meeting ocean, mounted on cream. *Reference for the zine's quietest spreads* — the photograph itself is the composition, the mat does the rest.

## What to extract from this expanded cluster

### Colors now represented across both batches
- Cream / off-white / warm-white ground (dominant throughout)
- Forest green in duotone-treated photography (original)
- Dusty navy / steel blue in information-heavy layouts (original)
- **Cyan-blue in torn-paper duotone — reference 31 only, for cinematic-memory context**
- **Purple/lavender as aura — reference 33, for spiritual-theoretical moments**
- Muted orange / red-brown as diagrammatic accents (originals 9, 14)
- Black for type and silhouettes

### Typography now represented
- Modest sans-serif for body text (references 7, 13, 18, 24, 28)
- Display sans for poster-scale moments (reference 9 — serif exception)
- **Typewriter-style mono for subtitle grammar and caption strips — references 24, 28, 30**
- Small mono uppercase for captions and labels (references 4, 8, 14, 36)
- **Handwriting as epistolary voice — references 22, 29, 33** (Register B permitted use)
- Handwriting as closing signature only — references 1, 32 (Register A strict use)

### Compositional moves that now repeat across both batches
- Single image + single phrase + cream (references 13, 15, 25, 36, 40)
- Image grid as catalog (references 7, 10, 20, 38)
- Composed portrait with callouts or annotations (references 21, 27)
- Postcard grammar — image + handwritten message (references 1, 22)
- Text-over-image as overlay moment (references 29, 30, 31)
- Layered cinematic composite (references 26, 32)
- Botanical specimen + photographic evidence (references 39, 17, 32)

### What NOT to extract (emphasized by the expansion)
- Full-color saturated flatlay photography
- Serif display type *as primary* (reference 9 and 31 use it contextually)
- Busy collage with tape, rotation, and overlay in combination
- White background with no warmth
- Lifestyle, Kinfolk, "cozy" register
- Scattered decorative doodles (reference 35's doodles are the *only* permitted use — framing a single Polaroid, not decorating a page)

## Handling these in Claude Design

These references supplement the primary upload (`design-system/visual-language-foundation.html`). They provide finished-work context for what the visual language looks like across a range of publications, formats, and the two registers.

**Important instruction for Claude Design extraction:** Do not collapse the two registers into a single "average" style. The visual language has a quiet chapbook mode (Register A — zine) and a cinematic-editorial composite mode (Register B — deck, film, social). When an asset is commissioned, the register should be selected by format. When in doubt, Register A is the default.

If Claude Design's extraction starts producing busy collage based on references 27, 31, 32, or 33 — remove those references from the upload and re-extract. They are included for specific limited uses, not as the default.

The strongest references for correctly extracting the system as a whole are **13, 15, 22, 25, 36, 40** — these six images show the center of the visual language across both registers.
