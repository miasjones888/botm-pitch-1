What stays true from the full plan

  • Collage-essay form, four positions held implicitly, no declared grammar
  • Character in two registers — cinematic + casual/webcam, voice always
  • Two artifacts per episode — film + companion printed essay
  • Tied to BOTM's literary fiction / contemporary fiction / Volume 0 lane — not whole catalog
  • Global register, not European; reading life of any woman, not aspirational Paris
  • Centennial as light context only, not frame
Field 3 source paragraph · keep visible while drafting
Sitting out on a balcony in Paris, watching the sitting reading and thinking and drinking coffee and smoking and writing in your journal while listening to music and perhaps talking passionately with a friend about the book you're reading and how it makes you feel — what you see vs. what they see, the poetry and prose it inspires in you, the change it inspires, how it touches your own life, how you see the world when you come out of the world within the book, how it immediately becomes nostalgic and fuzzy because you can never go back to the feeling of reading the book for the first time, how it feels to hold a book close to your heart, your mind, for it to inspire deep thought, feeling, and discussion, how it connects to the world.

BLOCK 2Sample episode sketch
60–75 min · writing mode
The hardest creative task. Doing it today while Field 3 energy is fresh protects every downstream block. Treatment, not script. 500–700 words total.
Minimum viable
List-form only: opening image (1 sentence), 4 drift bullets (1 sentence each), closing return (1 sentence), companion essay-object (1 sentence), three short cuts (one line each). ~200 words. Still works for Slide 3.

Structure

  • 2.1
    New Google Doc: "01-sample-episode-lost-lambs.gdoc". Paste the caught line from Block 1.2 at the top.
  • 2.2
    Write 1–2 sentences on why the line caught. For you, not a reader.
  • 2.3
    Opening image. 2–3 sentences. What does the viewer see first? Register, not detail. Where is the reader. What light. What sound.
  • 2.4
    Book introduced. 2–3 sentences. How does the book arrive — voiceover? A held shot of the cover? The caught line read aloud?
  • 2.5
    Drift movement 1. Paragraph. First association the book opens. An image, a memory, another text, a song.
  • 2.6
    Drift movement 2. Paragraph. Second tributary. Different register — if 1 was interior, 2 goes outward.
  • 2.7
    Drift movement 3. Paragraph. Where writing surfaces — a line of your own prose or poetry, or a fragment from another writer.
  • 2.8
    Drift movement 4. Paragraph. Social dimension — a conversation with a friend, a letter, something received.
  • 2.9
    Return. 2–3 sentences. Back to the book, the reader, the closing image or line. Settles, does not conclude.
  • 2.10
    Companion essay-object. 1 short paragraph. What is in print — a poem, a prose fragment, a letter, a collage of lines. Format (folded card, short zine, single page).
  • 2.11
    Three short cuts. Two lines each. Atmospheric (no voice) · voiced (single drift movement) · text-forward (fragment on image).
  • 2.12
    Read the whole sketch cold. Save. Close. Do not revise tonight.
BLOCK 3Field 3 first draft
45–60 min · writing mode
The main deliverable. Target 400–600 words. Four-move structure. Start from the balcony paragraph.
Minimum viable
300 words, three moves instead of four (drop Move 3 — naming the lane — it's implied in Moves 1 and 2 if the voice is right). Still credible.
  • 3.1
    New Google Doc: "02-field-3-draft.gdoc".
  • 3.2
    Paste the balcony paragraph at the top of the doc. Keep it visible the whole time.
  • 3.3
    Move 1 — Sensibility in motion. 2–3 opening sentences that put the reader inside the register. Pull language from the balcony paragraph. Do NOT open with "I propose" or "I want to make." Open with a sentence that sounds like the piece itself.
  • 3.4
    Move 2 — Define the form. 1 paragraph. What the piece is: monthly-viable collage-essay, film + companion printed essay. Drawn from Block 2. Clean and direct.
  • 3.5
    Move 3 — Name the lane. 1 paragraph. Who the piece is for — the literary fiction subset of BOTM's audience, the Volume 0 reader. Drop 2–3 author names from your taste cluster (Madeline Cash, Kiran Millwood Hargrave, Aja Gabel, Emma Cline).
  • 3.6
    Move 4 — Close with the ask. Pitching ONE piece first. Expansion named as possibility, not demand. End on a single line restating what the piece is.
  • 3.7
    Add a line at the end pointing to the deck: "A short deck supporting this pitch is here: [Drive link TBD tomorrow]."
  • 3.8
    Word count check: 400–600. Cut if over 700. Add one paragraph if under 300.
  • 3.9
    Read aloud once. Cut any sentence that sounds like pitch-deck corporate language. Keep voice.
  • 3.10
    Save. Walk away. Do not revise tonight.
Voice check
Read back the balcony paragraph. That is your voice. If Field 3 sounds more corporate than that paragraph, revise toward the paragraph. If it sounds more writerly than the paragraph, keep it — you have range above the baseline.

Slide 1 — Atmosphere (not a title slide)

JOB
Make reader feel the piece before any explanation.
CONTENT
One full-bleed or large image + one short phrase (5–10 words). No title. No logo. No deck name.
PHRASE OPTIONS

IMAGE
Personal photo or Canva free library — a hand with a book, an unstyled domestic interior, a window, a reader's shoulder. Not Kinfolk-perfect. Real.

Pitch do's and don'ts

Do
  • Open Field 3 with the sensibility in motion — not "I propose"
  • Name specific authors from your taste cluster
  • Keep the balcony paragraph's voice in your ear
  • Reference Volume 0 as adjacent register
  • Pitch ONE piece first; name expansion as possibility
  • Write in your actual sentence rhythm, not pitch-deck language
Don't
  • Open with "I want to make" or "My idea is"
  • Promise views, engagement, reach, or quantitative metrics
  • Lean on the centennial as primary frame
  • Use cozy / me-time / book-club framing
  • Use bullet lists inside Field 3
  • Describe the piece — evoke it
  • Include a headshot on Slide 5
  • Put "pitch deck" anywhere on Slide 1

Voice tuning — if Field 3 sounds off, try one of these

  • Open with an image or a scene instead of a concept
  • Cut the word "content" if it appears — replace with "work" or "piece"
  • Cut "audience" if it appears — replace with "reader" or "readers"
  • Cut any paragraph that starts with "I" three times in a row
  • Read the balcony paragraph aloud, then read your draft aloud. The gap between them is the edit.
