# AESTHETIC & ZINE DIRECTION — Found Document Project
*Visual, formal, and material direction for the eventual object. Pulled from your existing aesthetic systems and adapted to this material.*

---

## Anchoring tonal directive

From your established design vocabulary: **indifferent and accurate, not warm or whimsical.** That holds here exactly. The found document's clinical voice is itself indifferent and accurate. The aesthetic of the piece should not soften or aestheticize the medical material — it should match its register.

This means no:
- Watercolor washes, soft florals, hand-lettered "comforting" type
- Memorial-card iconography (doves, candles, scripture-style serif)
- Generic "grief design" — the muted earth-tones / lowercase-serif / linen-texture grief aesthetic that has become its own visual genre
- Photo treatments that romanticize the document
- Healing language anywhere in the design

What it should be:
- Document-as-document. Form-as-form. Letterhead. Logbook. Discharge paperwork.
- Cold typography meeting handmade marks
- The visual register of the file itself: black-and-white, sans serif, fixed-width where possible, the Google-Doc default
- Marks that look like marginalia, not like decoration

---

## The dominant aesthetic — three directions to choose from

These are not all of them. They are the three I would actually pursue. Pick one. Don't blend.

### Direction A — *The Hospital Document*

Treat the zine as if it were a hospital discharge packet. Manila envelope or similar. Inside: the piece is laid out on photocopied-looking paper. Times New Roman or Courier where the original document's voice is being used. Sans serif (Helvetica, Arial, IBM Plex) for your voice. The typography itself does the voice-marking work.

Visual elements:
- Stamped ink marks (date stamps, "RECEIVED," "CONFIRMED," "DISCHARGE")
- Vital sign blocks reproduced as tables, with the medical formatting intact
- Signature lines, blank fields, form numbers
- Carbon-copy purple where useful
- Photocopier artifacts: dust marks, paper edges, faint banding
- Page numbers in form-style: "Page 1 of —" with the total left blank
- ID-band imagery (the plastic bracelet at the wrist)
- Possibly: the back covers of pages used for your handwritten responses, scanned in

**Reference points:** the Anne Carson *Nox* facsimile feel (handmade, scanned, artifact-heavy); medical record fan-art (rare but exists); the *Raised by Wolves* discharge document zine genre; estate paperwork.

**Best fit if:** you want the zine to feel like the document arrived in a box of paperwork after someone died. The reader handles it like inherited material.

### Direction B — *The Net Art / ASCII Field Guide*

Pulls directly from the aesthetic framework you've already developed for the project-tracking app — net art / web 1.0 / ASCII forest logic / "indifferent and accurate." This treats the document as data to be parsed, archived, and re-rendered through a low-fi digital register.

Visual elements:
- Monospace throughout (IBM Plex Mono, JetBrains Mono, Berkeley Mono)
- ASCII rule lines as section dividers: `------- 11:12 am -------`
- Box-drawing characters for vital sign blocks, family trees, room maps
- File-tree structures showing how the document organizes itself
- Stark 1-bit imagery (no grayscale): inked diagrams, drawn-by-hand-then-scanned-to-1-bit illustrations
- Hyperlinks shown as `<<linked text>>` in monospace, even when not linked
- Footnotes in `[1]` format
- Section headers as filenames: `dad-20-oct-nov-2024.txt`, `notes/family-letter.md`, `reaction/01.md`
- Possibly: a faux table-of-contents shown as a directory listing

**Reference points:** Olia Lialina's net.art; geocities; *House of Leaves* footnoting; Are.na as form; the "computer-as-grief-archive" feel; recent text-mode zines (e.g. anything from Topos Press); your own ASCII forest direction.

**Best fit if:** you want the document framed as an *artifact extracted from a system*. The cold of the medium underscoring the cold of the material. Most aligned with your existing visual systems.

### Direction C — *The Memorial Object*

Anti-decorative but artifact-heavy. Built like an object you find at an estate sale, not a designed zine. Mixed media. Hand-assembled. Closer to Carson's *Nox* than to print zine convention.

Visual elements:
- Unbleached or off-white paper. Possibly newsprint.
- Typewriter typography (real or convincingly digital)
- Handwritten elements scanned in — your handwriting in margins, on overlays, on tipped-in pages
- Photographs that are not photographs of people: an empty hospital bed, a phone screen showing read receipts, a Ring doorbell, a microwave dinner, a half-open mail slot, a window with a view of the ocean. *Specifically not faces. Specifically not the document's family.*
- Folded inserts (a vital sign chart you unfold)
- Tipped-in elements (a pasted-in receipt; a photocopied prescription label)
- Asymmetrical layout, hand-trimmed feel
- The cover is the closest thing to a title and gives almost nothing away

**Reference points:** *Nox* (Anne Carson). *The Mezzanine* footnotes meet *The Family of Man*. Sophie Calle's *Take Care of Yourself*. Christian Boltanski's archives. The aesthetic of inheritance.

**Best fit if:** you want the piece to be a *thing*, not a publication. Most ambitious. Most expensive. Probably v2, not v1.

---

## My recommendation for v1

**Direction B (Net Art / ASCII Field Guide)**, for these specific reasons:

1. It's already your aesthetic. You've built tooling and visual logic around it. You won't be inventing a system; you'll be deploying one you have.
2. It produces in Figma → PDF cleanly. No physical fabrication required for v1. The completion floor is reachable.
3. It carries the "indifferent and accurate" tone better than Direction A (which can drift toward kitschy medical-LARP) or Direction C (which is beautiful but expensive in time/cost and risks aestheticizing the material).
4. Net art's foundational gesture — *the document made strange by its medium* — is exactly what your piece is doing.
5. The vital sign blocks, the timestamped logs, the file-tree organization of the document, the read-receipts-as-evidence motif — all of these are *natively at home* in the net art / monospace register.

Direction A is the second choice if Direction B feels too distancing. Direction C is the v2 dream.

## Specific design moves (Direction B-flavored)

If you go Direction B, here are concrete visual moves to consider. These are options, not requirements.

### Typography
- **Body text:** monospace throughout. IBM Plex Mono is excellent and free. Berkeley Mono if you want to license it.
- **Voice differentiation by weight, not font:** the document's voice in regular weight; your voice in lighter (book/light) or with a single-character indentation; caretaker voice in italic monospace. Don't switch families.
- **No serif faces anywhere.** Even for emphasis.
- **Alignment: ragged right.** Not justified. Justified monospace creates rivers.

### Color
- **Primary palette:** black, off-white (#F5F2EA or similar warm cream), one accent color used very sparingly.
- **Suggested accent:** a single washed-out teal (your favorite color, mentioned in your established preferences) used for ONE thing only — perhaps just for date stamps, or just for your own marginalia. The discipline of one accent.
- **Avoid:** red. The temptation will be to use red for emphasis or alarm. It will look like medical TV. Stay achromatic.

### Structural elements
- **Section headers as filenames:** `discovery/10-23-24.log`, `voice/jay.txt`, `voice/caretaker.txt`, `letter/dear-family.eml`, `reaction/01.md`
- **ASCII rule lines:** `─────────` or `─ ─ ─ ─ ─` between sections
- **Vital sign blocks:** preserved as tables or as monospace-aligned blocks that look like terminal output
- **The MRI line:** isolated. Whitespace around it. Possibly its own page.
- **The handgun gesture line:** isolated. Possibly its own page. Possibly a black page with white text.
- **The closing letter:** rendered as an email, with a header (`From: Amy / To: Family / Subject: Update on Dad / Date: Nov 7 2024`)
- **The final period:** literal. Final page. White space above. `.` Bottom of page. Done.

### Imagery
- **Almost no photographic imagery.** Photos pull the reader toward the literal family and away from the form.
- **Acceptable image types:** ASCII diagrams (a floor plan of the house? a timeline of falls? a list of devices?). Screen grabs of the kind of UI surveillance the family used (read receipts, Find My, Ring app — not actually theirs; reproductions / mockups). Scanned handwriting (yours). 1-bit illustrations.
- **One unifying recurring image:** a small pixel/ASCII glyph that appears at the start of each section. Possibly: `[ ]` (an unchecked box, for the unkept conversations). Possibly: a single-line ECG trace. Possibly: nothing. **Restraint is the aesthetic.**

### Cover
- The cover is a problem to solve last.
- Best move: looks nothing like a "grief book." Looks like a folder, or a file, or a notice. Title, if any, in the same monospace as the body. Possibly: just a filename. Possibly: just a date range. Possibly: no title at all, just the cover format.

## Production stack (when you reach Phase 10)

Per your build stack as of April 20, 2026:

- **Layout/design:** Figma. This is its job. The whole zine can live as one Figma file.
- **Type setup:** install IBM Plex Mono locally, set up text styles in Figma early
- **PDF export:** Figma → PDF directly. No InDesign needed for a zine of this scale.
- **Print-ready PDF (if printing):** add bleeds in Figma; export with crop marks. Send to a riso printer (recommended for this aesthetic — riso's grain matches the indifferent/accurate register) or a digital print shop. Riso suggestions: People's Press, Issue Press, Cold Cube Press (US-based options). Local LA: Tiny Splendor, Mama Press.
- **Distribution PDF:** Canva is fine if you want to make a clean download version. Skip if Figma export is sufficient.
- **Avoid Claude Design for this** — there is no code↔design loop here, so it's not the right tool. Pure layout work belongs in Figma.

## Reference moodboard (text-only, build the actual visual board yourself)

Things to look at before you design:

- **Anne Carson, *Nox*** — handle a copy if you can. The accordion fold is the lesson.
- **Olia Lialina, *My Boyfriend Came Back from the War* (1996)** — net.art canon. The browser-as-form move.
- **Are.na as platform** — not a single artist; the platform's aesthetic. Cold, indexical, modular.
- **JODI.org** — net art's most uncomfortable practitioner. You don't have to like it. Notice what it does.
- **Sophie Calle, *Take Care of Yourself*** — the "found document interpreted by 107 women" project. Closest conceptual cousin to what you might be making.
- **The Topos Press catalog** — text-mode zines, monospace publishing. Aesthetic peer.
- **Christian Bök, *Eunoia*** — constraint as form, monospace publishing.
- **Renee Gladman, *Calamities*** — fragment-form, cold register, brilliant.
- **Bhanu Kapil, *Ban en Banlieue*** — failed novel as published form. The "this is a draft, this is a notebook, this is the book" gesture.
- **The aesthetic of estate paperwork** — actual: medical bills, hospital discharge packets, prescription labels, receipts. The visual register of the literal documents that surround a death. Worth photographing some if you have access.
- **Your own existing ASCII forest mood and project-tracking-app aesthetic framework** — this piece can live in the same visual world.

## Format and dimensions

For v1, suggested:

- **Format:** PDF zine, single-file, side-stapled or saddle-stitched if printed
- **Dimensions:** 5.5" × 8.5" (half-letter folded — most economical for printing on letter stock) OR 5" × 8" (standard digest) OR 6" × 9" (trade)
- **Page count:** 16, 20, 24, or 32 pages. (Multiples of 4 for saddle-stitch.) v1 ceiling: 32. Try for 20.
- **Reading time:** 10–25 minutes. Sit-with-it object, not a book.

---

## What NOT to do (visual)

- No watercolor anything
- No script or hand-lettered titles
- No "in loving memory" framing devices
- No imagery of: hospital beds with people in them, hands holding hands, sunsets, doves, candles, religious symbols, butterflies, trees with leaves falling
- No quote pulled out and centered with a designerly drop cap
- No font that's having a personality
- No three-column magazine layout
- No "designed for the kindle" vibe
- No softening of the document's medical voice through visual treatment
- **No making this beautiful.** The piece can be elegant. It cannot be beautiful. Beauty would be a betrayal.

## Optional: zine-as-web-object alternative

If physical or PDF-zine doesn't excite you, a parallel direction worth flagging:

The piece could live as a **single-page web object**. Scrolling. Monospace. Dark mode option. The voice shifts handled with subtle color/weight changes. The vital sign blocks rendered as actual `<table>` HTML. The MRI line gets its own viewport-height block. The final period is a `<div>` with one character.

If you go this route: deploy on Vercel, single static file, no framework needed. Could be 2–3 sessions of work after the writing is done. Could share via URL. Could be retired into a screenshot archive afterwards.

This crosses into your build-art interests but does not require a code↔design loop, so Figma → handwritten HTML/CSS → Vercel is the cleanest path.

**This is a v2 option, not v1.** v1 is the printed/PDF zine. Web version is a possible second life.

---

## One last directive

The aesthetic decisions cannot save the writing and they cannot kill it. They can only honor or betray it. The right design is the one that disappears — the reader feels the document, not the layout. *Indifferent and accurate.* Stay there.

End of aesthetic direction.
