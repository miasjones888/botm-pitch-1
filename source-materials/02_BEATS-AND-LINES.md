# BEATS & LINES — Found Document Extraction
*Verbatim pulls from the source document, sorted for creative use.*

A note on selection: these are the lines and moments that have literary charge — image, music, repetition, accidental poetry, structural rhythm, the gap between clinical voice and human disaster. They are not always the "important" lines. Often the literary line is the throwaway one. The dad eating a receipt. The fork backwards. *Tastes like Heaven.*

The clinical voice and the family voice run parallel through the document and almost never meet. That tension is the formal opportunity.

## II. THE STRUCTURAL BEATS — narrative spine

The document has an actual three-act structure. This is unusual for a real-time medical log.

**Act I — Discovery and Crisis (Oct 23–24)**
- Daughter notices texts unread.
- Police welfare check.
- Found on the floor in laundry room.
- Sepsis and rhabdo concerns.
- Mouth crusted with waxy mucus, family removes it themselves.
- First *Feels like Heaven*.

**Act II — Recognition and Decline (Oct 25–30)**
- Confusion deepens.
- He doesn't recognize his children.
- The handgun gesture.
- Sodium too high, then too low.
- Misidentifications: Jay as Bud, Annie as Joanie.
- Photos shown — does not recognize.
- The tries-to-eat-the-phone moment.
- Hospital errors accumulate (wrong weight, wrong meals, wrong unauthorized surgery).
- MRI confirms major stroke.
- Decision to transfer.

**Act III — Transfer and Hospice (Oct 31–Nov 7)**
- Sign out AMA.
- Drive from Vegas to Orange County with Jay in the medical transport.
- St. Joseph's intake.
- Hospice decision.
- Newport Beach rented home, ocean view.
- The family letter.
- The period at the end of the letter.

If the piece uses a three-part structure, these are the three parts. If it doesn't, this is what it's resisting.

## III. THE RECURRING ELEMENTS — words/phrases that pattern

These repeat across days and voices. Patterning is form. Mark them and let them carry.

| Element | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **"Tastes like Heaven" / "Feels like Heaven"** | The chorus. Hospice subtext. |
| **"Where's Jay?" (asked of Jay)** | The recursive failure of recognition. Comic and devastating. |

| **"Sleeping" / "Dozed off" / "Fell back asleep"** | He's mostly absent in his own document. |
| **The puree diet** | The reduction. Adult food → soft food → liquid → IV. |
| **The phone — read receipts, Find My, Ring, Life Alert** | Every surveillance device the family installed to keep him alive failed. |
| **Diapers, briefs, soiling** | The reversal of care. The same daughters who were once changed by him. |

| **"He doesn't remember this conversation happening"** | Said multiple times. The grief-of-the-moment is also forgotten. |

## IV. THE GAPS — what is not in the document

What the document refuses to say is as charged as what it says. These are openings for your voice.

- **The mother is barely present.** "Where's your mom" is asked twice. Mimi/Mehmee is named once. She's the absence around which everything organizes. If Mimi was the wife — what happened? Divorce? Death? The document never says. *Your* mother situation could enter through this gap.
- **The brother (Jim) — psychotic, tried to kill the father, deceased.** One sentence. A whole inheritance of mental illness compressed into a parenthetical. The bipolar lineage. *Your* father situation could enter here.
- **What Dad was like before.** No backstory. We meet him in his decline. We never know what kind of father he was. The cruelty (or the love) that produced these adult children is invisible. The reader has to construct it from how they show up.
- **What the children feel.** Jay is the closest to a narrator and even he stays operational. Annie cries once (off-page, implied). Amy never visibly reacts. The flatness is doing work — but it's also a refusal. *Your* feeling could occupy this absence.
- **The cause of the fall.** Never resolved. The kidney doctor says he can't have been down that long. The evidence says he was. This is the document's central mystery and it stays a mystery.
- **What he did with the gun gesture.** The line is reported flat. No one in the document responds to it.
- **What he wanted.** December 2023, they tried to move him home. He refused. The document calls this "frustrating" but doesn't sit with what it meant. He chose this. That's the unspoken thing.

## V. THE FORM-CHARGED ELEMENTS — things that carry their own structure

Pieces of the document that already have form. You can lift these and use them as-is, or use their structure.

1. **The vital sign line.** *"114/61 / Pulse 67 / Oxygen: 94% / Temp 36.6"* — has its own line breaks already. Found ode form.
2. **The caretaker time-stamp log.** *6:50am: ... / 7:17am: ... / 7:49am: ...* — the relentless cadence. Like a Shaker hymnal of attention.
3. **The MRI revelation** — single line buried mid-log: *"11:12 am: internal medicine doctor came in with MRI results. Client had stroke."* The flatness of the news.
4. **The discovery scene inventory** — list form, no main verb. *"TV still on, blood on the floor, hardened feces..."*
5. **The "Note:" interruptions** — Jay breaking out of timestamp form to add forensic context. The narrator showing himself.
6. **The closing letter** — formal letter form, embedded in a medical log. The genre shift is the climax.
7. **The signature block** — *"Amy, Annie, and Jay"* — three names ordered by some logic (alphabetical? age? other?). The last words.
8. **The period after "With love and gratitude,"** — the document ends with a period on its own line, after the names. *"."* The literal full stop.

## VI. RESONANCE LINES — beats that hit your specific material

Marked carefully. These are *for you*, not for the piece (necessarily).

- **"His manic depressive disorder (Bipolar) disallows him from feeling feelings."** Bipolar father. Feeling refusal. Hard exterior the moment vulnerability shows. → your father.
- **"He refuses help and lies about events like these, trying to hide any concerns."** The stranger's father. Also potentially: your father. Also the grandfather pattern.
- **"This visit was primarily focused on selling him on the dream of moving back home and leaving Vegas. Despite our best efforts and arrangements set as far as even finding his new replacement home in Huntington Beach, he declined."** The failure of the rescue. The choice of decline. The adult child who can't override the parent. → grandfather; → your withdrawal from your father.
- **The sequence of failed care:** medications not taken → bills unpaid → service cut off → fall → discovery. The slow self-destruction that the watching daughters can't intervene in. → your mother caretaking failure. → the question of how much agency the dying have.
- **"He makes funny comments to the nurses and is his sarcastic self."** The cruel man's sense of humor surviving everything else. → grandfather.
- **"Dad does not call and does not text."** → your father.
- **The whole December 2023 visit** — the rescue attempt that doesn't take. → if your withdrawal from your father has had any rescue-attempt phase, this is the mirror.

These do not need to be in the piece. Naming them now is structural — it tells you where the live wires are so you can decide whether to touch them or route around.
