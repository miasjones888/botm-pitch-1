Marginalia — what the work is
a monthly piece · film + printed essay

The reading life of any woman, anywhere — filmed and written with craft.

form

A monthly collage-essay held by a characterized reader-figure. One book as occasion, the reading as subject. A 6–9 minute film, collaged across image, voice, writing, and sound. A companion printed essay that ships in the BOTM box. Three short cuts for social.

treatment · drift movements
drift 01 · interior
The line that catches

Open on a held shot — a hand on the book, a window beyond. The voice reads one line aloud. The line opens the room.

drift 02 · outward
First tributary

An association the book opens — a memory, an image, another text. The film moves away from the book and back to it.

drift 03 · written
Where writing surfaces

A line of original prose appears on screen — written in response, not commentary. The reader becomes a writer for a moment.

drift 04 · social
A conversation

A fragment of a letter, a recorded voice memo from a friend. Reading as something held between people.

Companion essay-object A printed insert in the BOTM box — structured as event-score / spatial poem. Roughly the size of a postcard, printed on uncoated stock. Volume-Ø adjacent in publishing register.
what gets made — across surfaces

Five surfaces, one piece.

Slide 05 · build notes — Postcard variant

Closing as artifact — the back of a postcard.

Editorial move: the slide enacts the work it pitches. Marginalia includes printed essay-objects in the BOTM box; the closing slide is itself one of those objects. Left side = message side with handwritten closing line and signature. Right side = address-block formatted bio with postage stamp + postmark.

Lavender: the postage stamp area top-right is the lavender moment for this slide. Pale lavender fill, lavender border, navy "M." monogram inside.

Hand: the closing line itself is set in Reenie Beanie at large scale, with handwritten "— with thanks, Mia" sign-off. The hand is doing the closing work, not annotation work. This is the one slide where hand goes large.

Bio: reformatted as an address block. "To Book of the Month" / "From Mia Jones, San Diego, CA" / role / contact lines. Reads like a real postcard back, not a deck "About me" slide.
