section 01

Foundations — principles & through-line

What this design system is for, what it's not for, and the four principles every applied artifact follows.

An intellectually serious zine that lives between the contemporary literary magazine, the field guide, the experimental score, and the parts of the early internet that raised us — produced with editorial restraint that sits comfortably alongside Volume 0.

Principles

01 · Editorial restraint over decorative density

Generous negative space. Few elements per page, each placed with intent. When in doubt, cut. The work should look like it was art-directed, not assembled. Restraint signals confidence.

02 · Sans as voice, mono as architecture

General Sans carries the working voice — body, display, headers, the language the reader is meant to hear. IBM Plex Mono is the architecture around the voice — metadata, captions, labels, file names, the scaffolding that holds the page. Two faces, two clear jobs.

03 · Personality in two small places

The lavender accent appears once per artifact. A simulated handwritten annotation appears once per artifact. These are signatures, not patterns. Used sparingly, they identify the work; used everywhere, they become noise.

04 · BOTM-resonant, not BOTM-derivative

The cream and ink baseline sits in conversation with BOTM's existing visual identity (warm cream, near-black, occasional cobalt). The dusty navy and soft lavender accents are the creative-direction signature — recognizably not their house style, but not jarring against it.

What this system is not

  • Not a magazine layout. Density and column structure belong to magazines; this is zine-spread sensibility, professionally executed.
  • Not a corporate brand guide. There is no logo system, no scaled mark, no app-screen treatment.
  • Not a moodboard. Every element here is functional — tokens that get applied, components that get reused.
  • Not exhaustive. This is a working system for one pitch, not a library for ongoing brand work.

Color rules

  • Never use pure white (#FFFFFF) or pure black (#000000). Both break the warm-alive baseline.
  • Lavender appears once per artifact, maximum. Used everywhere, it becomes kitsch. Used sparingly, it identifies the work.
  • Navy carries all primary accent work. If an element needs accent emphasis and it's not the lavender moment, it's navy.
  • Photographic moments may include iridescence as content. Oil-on-water, mother-of-pearl, sunlight on glass — held in the image, not added as graphic treatment.
  • BOTM resonance, not derivative. Cream + ink + occasional blue echoes BOTM's house language. Lavender + dusty navy is the signature.

Spatial system — spacing, rules, density

The grid is invisible, but it exists. Spacing is generous. Rules carry hierarchy. Density varies by artifact.

4px base unit. Used for all margins, padding, gaps. Named for hierarchy, not for size.

Soft dividers within components. Visually quieter than thin rule. Indicates continuation, not break.

Density principles

  • Slide 1 (atmosphere) — very low density. One image, one phrase. Cream carries most of the page.
  • Slide 2 (concept) — medium density. Title, lift quote, body paragraph, breath room.
  • Slide 3 (sample episode) — highest density. Multi-fragment grid, image cells, captions. Earned density — the slide that proves the work.
  • Slide 4 (system) — medium density. Five-stop diagram with breath between elements.
  • Slide 5 (close) — low density. Bio + closing line. Returns to breath.

section 06

Voice & register — how this thing sounds

Visual register and verbal register are the same thing, expressed in different mediums. The voice rules apply to all written language across all artifacts.

The register, in one paragraph

Direct, considered, unfussy. Sentences earn their length. Trust the reader. Avoid pitch-deck corporate language at all costs — "synergy," "leverage," "engagement metrics," "audience" used as a faceless mass. Speak about readers, not users; about the work, not content; about readers carrying a book around all month, not monthly active engagement.

Voice rules

  • Open with sensibility, not concept. The first sentence puts the reader inside the register. "I propose to make..." is dead on arrival.
  • Use the word reader when you mean reader. Never user, audience, consumer, viewer.
  • Use the word work when you mean work. Never content, asset, deliverable, IP.
  • Specifics over abstractions. "A balcony in Paris, smoking, talking to a friend" beats "an immersive contemplative reading experience."
  • Name authors and books by name. Madeline Cash, Aja Gabel, Kiran Millwood Hargrave, Emma Cline. Specificity is credibility.
  • Cut every adverb that doesn't earn its place. Especially really, truly, deeply, profoundly.
  • End on a settled line, not a conclusive one. The piece is contemplative; the closing line should rest, not declare.

One pull-quote, anchoring the voice

Sitting out on a balcony in Paris, watching the sitting reading and thinking and drinking coffee and smoking and writing in your journal — how it feels to hold a book close to your heart, your mind, for it to inspire deep thought, feeling, and discussion, how it connects to the world.
section 07

Do's & don'ts

The shortest, most-violatable rules. Reference before every applied build.

Do
  • Lead with breath. Slide 1 should hold one image and one short phrase, with cream carrying most of the page.
  • Use sans for all primary working type — body, display, headers.
  • Use mono for all metadata, captions, labels, file names, section markers.
  • Cite specific authors, specific books, specific lines.
  • Pair every image with a mono micro-caption.
  • Allow asymmetry — nothing needs to be centered.
  • Use the lavender once per artifact, deliberately.
  • Use the handwritten annotation once per artifact, deliberately.
Don't
  • Use pure white or pure black anywhere.
  • Use lavender on more than one element per artifact.
  • Center-align everything. Asymmetry signals art-direction; symmetry signals corporate template.
  • Open Field 3 with "I propose," "I want to make," or "My idea is."
  • Use any sans-serif other than General Sans. No Inter, no system sans, no Helvetica fallback.
  • Use serif typefaces anywhere in the pitch.
  • Drop in stock photography. If a real image isn't available, use a captioned placeholder frame.
  • Use the words content, audience, deliverable, asset, leverage, synergy anywhere.
  • Include a headshot of Mia. The deck does not contain a headshot. The bio carries credibility.
  • Add gradients, drop shadows, soft glows, or any 2010s product-design effects.
section 08

Applied — where this gets used

The four pitch artifacts that consume this design system, and how each one applies the tokens.

01 · Pitch deck (5 slides)

PDF, hosted on Drive, link embedded in Field 3. Built in Canva using these tokens. Each slide applies the system to a different density level (see Spatial — Density principles).

02 · Field 3 written pitch

Lives inline in the application as plain text. The voice rules and register principles apply — even though typography doesn't render in the form field, the writing embodies the system.

03 · Video sensibility one-sheet

One page. Image-frame grid (placeholder-captioned), single pull-quote describing the film's air, brief paragraph of voice/treatment notes. Uses the standard component vocabulary at low density.

04 · Zine spread one-sheet

One double-page spread mockup of the companion essay-object, structured as event-score / spatial poem. Uses sans and mono together, plus the option of a concrete-poetry moment as the form's signature.

All four artifacts consume the same token system. If a future BOTM piece extends the work, the system extends with it — same colors, same faces, same components, same voice.
